# Product contract

Target edition: 16 October 2026. Uptime is a complete website uptime and API monitoring product for a US small-business site or application operator replacing UptimeRobot, Pingdom (SolarWinds), Better Stack Uptime or Site24x7, with the monitoring-as-code and transaction depth buyers seek from Checkly, OpenStatus and Hyperping. Prices/budgets are USD, phone defaults are US numbers, and schedules/reports support US time zones and daylight saving.

The product discovers, operates and maintains trustworthy monitoring of the actual customer outcome: HTTP/API, every named protocol below, job heartbeats, multi-region and private checks, browser transactions, expiring assets, actionable alerts and reporting. Oncall owns full responder coordination and Lamp owns customer-facing status communication; their integrations are part of Uptime's monitoring job. No gap in implementation reduces this destination.

This is a target contract, not a release verdict. Every named clause states the buyer behavior and its acceptance journey. The final table distinguishes done, partial and missing at current code; a cited local test does not establish hosted placement, live migration, provider receipt or accessibility certification. Existing clause IDs and the owner-accepted 13 October archive policy and 16 October three-second readiness budget are preserved. This documentation round changes no homepage, interface or runtime behavior.

## Existing guarantees and supported customer journeys

- **HTTP-001 — Signed HTTP monitoring.** Public hosted HTTP(S) and keyword checks run in the Cloudflare Worker; private checks use the separately scoped Node runner. They retain the exact immutable assertion bundle and bounded transport evidence, and require authenticated claims and signed results; the control plane must reject contradictory completion. Hosted Worker checks leave through Cloudflare's shared egress and have no dedicated source address; published allowlistable probe addresses and probe identification are PROBE-002's. See [Integrity and authority](#integrity-and-authority) and [Network and credentials](#network-and-credentials). **Acceptance evidence:** Create an HTTP(S) and keyword monitor through the owner API, run scheduled hosted and signed runner work against a controlled HTTPS service, fail its status/text, recover it and inspect the immutable assertions and transport evidence.

- **EVIDENCE-001 — Immutable scheduled evidence.** Definitions, scheduled slots and every retry attempt retain revision, policy, fixed selected probes, runner identity, timings, status, assertions and error category; retries never erase failures, and observation/state/outbox commit atomically. See [Evidence and state](#evidence-and-state) and [Integrity and authority](#integrity-and-authority). **Acceptance evidence:** Through signed claim/result routes submit failure, retry, late and duplicate attempts, restart, and read history; each attempt and its revision remains identifiable and observation/state/outbox either all commit or none do.

- **STATE-001 — Fixed coverage and incident hysteresis.** Missing or expired passing coverage is Unknown, partial failure stays visible, independent-vantage agreement and scheduled confirmation determine Failing, and complete fresh scheduled passing evidence plus recovery threshold is required for recovery; manual checks and maintenance never rewrite observations or scheduled policy. See [Evidence and state](#evidence-and-state). **Acceptance evidence:** Run two assigned network vantages through partial, missing, stale, failing and recovering slots; inspect Unknown/Degraded/Failing and thresholds. A manual pass, absent probe or maintenance expiry cannot fabricate recovery.

- **AUTHORITY-001 — Server-side scoped authority.** Every private document, read, mutation and export requires server-side operator or scoped runner authority, missing configuration fails closed, browser mutations enforce configured origin, and expired/foreign attempts cannot alter current evidence, and duplicate attempt identities cannot overwrite it; a signature identifies a key holder rather than proving truthful observation. See [Integrity and authority](#integrity-and-authority). **Acceptance evidence:** Open private documents and call native/framework APIs while signed out, revoked, cross-origin or using a foreign/expired runner signature; require refusal with no product writes, then succeed with the authorized session or scoped runner.

- **NETWORK-001 — Pinned bounded destination execution.** Every public DNS answer and redirect hop is validated fail closed; private-runner numeric sockets preserve Host and TLS identity, hosted checks use Cloudflare’s public Internet fetch transport, unsafe/mixed addresses and HTTPS downgrade are rejected, cross-origin redirects strip request authority, and synthetic grants and protected origin-bound local credential files retain their separate custody limits. The PROBE-002 identification header follows these credential rules and is stripped on cross-origin redirects. See [Network and credentials](#network-and-credentials). **Acceptance evidence:** Exercise real controlled HTTP/TLS endpoints with mixed DNS, rebinding, hostname mismatch, redirects, downgrade, forbidden addresses, credentials and execution bounds; inspect requests and prove rejected destinations receive no connection or authority.

- **HEARTBEAT-001 — Revision-scoped receipt evaluation.** Hashed owner-issued tokens and, in the header form, increasing sequences protect server-time receipts, ingress cannot advance health, and scheduled signed completion must match its exact installation-local claim snapshot; stale, future, paused, foreign-revision or imported receipts cannot establish Healthy. These rules bind every receipt form of the buyer-facing job heartbeat in HEARTBEAT-002. See [Network and credentials](#network-and-credentials). **Acceptance evidence:** Issue and rotate a heartbeat token through the owner door, send increasing receipts, miss one, and evaluate scheduled signed snapshots; replay, old tokens, imported or foreign-revision receipts cannot change current health.

- **ARCHIVE-001 — Inactive validated restore.** Bounded versioned archives require integrity and semantic validation before transactional restore into empty product data, preserve historical evidence, erasure receipts and the permanent anonymised action audit, create paused current revisions, interrupt leases and quarantine alerts; installation-local sessions, keys, grants, request credential values, heartbeat authority, contact, webhook and Slack URLs, and signing keys are excluded; destination definitions and retained notification histories are portable, and imported evidence cannot establish current health or replay events. The source erasure journal fences copies made before a later erasure when that journal accompanies the restore. A local CLI receiving checkout must run setup first to establish its owner and settings. The owner accepted this installation-authority and endpoint-credential archive policy on 2026-10-13; it is an intentional replacement, not BASE-DATA-001 equivalence. See [Portability and retention](#portability-and-retention) and [Integrity and authority](#integrity-and-authority). **Acceptance evidence:** Export a populated installation, establish a different receiving owner, import into empty product data, then export/read history. Definitions are paused, leases interrupted, delivery quarantined, secrets/authority absent, history retained and later erasure journals enforced; corrupt/nonempty imports write nothing.

- **IMPORT-001 — Reviewed definition files and inactive imports.** A versioned definition file (2 MiB, 500 monitors, UTF-8 JSON, editor fields only, no request secrets or installation authority) or saved UptimeRobot v3 monitor pages are previewed without writes as create, unchanged, change, refused or unsupported per entry with a plan digest, validated by the editor's own validation; apply refuses a digest that no longer matches and otherwise writes the whole plan in one transaction as new paused monitors or new revisions that keep enabled, maintenance and runner assignments; nothing in a file enables a monitor, issues a heartbeat token, schedules a notification or touches a destination. Each apply is a batch; undo pauses what it created and restores what it changed while their revisions are unchanged, reports the rest and never removes history. Batches travel in archives as restored history without authority. Through this definition-file door a log export is recognised and counted only. Storing it as labelled source history for reports is HISTORY-001's migration import, and no imported history ever becomes evidence. Unmeasured: no real UptimeRobot export and no hosted run. See [Definition files and imports](#definition-files-and-imports). **Acceptance evidence:** Preview, apply, export and undo definition JSON and saved UptimeRobot pages through the owner routes and CLI. Malformed/hostile/secret-bearing inputs and stale digests write nothing; creations stay paused, changes keep operational state, unsupported fields are named and history is never made current evidence.

- **RETENTION-001 — Explicit storage custody.** Product and observation metadata and legacy development sign-in records have no automatic age/count deletion; operator erasure removes captures for the associated operator, and local reset clears the installation. Expired sessions, authentication/public rate-limit state and short-lived login-attempt counters are cleaned up. Expired or confirmed alert-drill links lose their capability material while drill history stays; retired destination secrets are scrubbed only after owner rotation/deletion and in-flight sends finish; a proven-unsent integration-attempt reservation can be removed while its delivery history remains. Other expired operational records, including discovery jobs, change status and remain stored unless an owner erases the associated entity or resets the installation. Raw target response bodies and probe-request secrets are not retained in observations; the only target content kept is EVIDENCE-002's bounded, redacted, access-controlled failure snapshot, stored apart from observations under owner-set retention, carried in archives and erased with its monitor or on owner request. Webhook and Slack endpoint URLs and signing keys remain installation-local, available email addresses travel as destination definitions, view/archive bounds are not deletion or capacity claims, and retention changes must never silently improve the fixed denominator or historical health. See [Portability and retention](#portability-and-retention). **Acceptance evidence:** Populate product, authentication, rate-limit, drill, credential and attempt records; run expiry, maintenance and restart through runtime doors and compare surviving identities. Only declared transient data/secret material disappears; no cleanup improves uptime or removes retained product history.

- **ERASURE-001 — Operator-directed entity erasure.** An owner can erase a monitor with its evidence and dependent notification/heartbeat records, a destination with its credential copies, or an operator while another active owner remains. Monitor erasure requires typing the current monitor name. Each completed erasure returns a receipt with entity kind, per-table counts and completion time, and returns it only after the transaction containing the erasure, audit entry and portability fence succeeds. Requests based on stale monitor or destination state cannot re-create erased rows. The last active owner cannot be erased: local installations use `rer reset --yes`, while hosted installations require replacement by redeployment. Uptime has no R2 upload feature, so product erasure has no uploaded attachments to remove. Already downloaded archives, messages already sent to external recipients and requests already in progress remain outside local erasure. See [Erasure and audit](#erasure-and-audit). **Acceptance evidence:** Erase each supported entity through the owner API, including stale concurrent requests and the original owner with a successor. Inspect the atomic per-table receipt, audit and journal; the last owner is protected and an old archive with the later journal cannot resurrect erased records.

- **AUDIT-001 — Durable anonymised audit.** Destructive actions, automated notification attempts and operator authentication-mail sends record actor or feature, time, action, subject kind and an opaque subject reference. Audit history is portable and survives erasure of the subject; erasing an operator anonymises its prior actor references. The audit contains no monitor names, target URLs, email addresses or destination URLs. See [Erasure and audit](#erasure-and-audit) and [Portability and retention](#portability-and-retention). **Acceptance evidence:** Perform destructive actions, notification attempts and authentication-mail attempts through public doors, erase an actor and round-trip an archive; inspect durable opaque subjects and anonymized actors without planted names, addresses, URLs or secrets.

- **PRESENCE-001 — Contact separate from health.** Only accepted scoped claims and signed results refresh server-time contact, the 120-second freshness threshold never proves process liveness or target health, and restart, unavailable reads and archive omission preserve that distinction. See [Contact, interface and delivery](#contact-interface-and-delivery). **Acceptance evidence:** Read Never seen, make accepted idle claims/results, age contact, reject foreign requests and restart. Reads and health checks do not refresh contact; unavailable reads and restore cannot imply runner or target health.

- **INTERFACE-001 — Honest durable operator feedback.** Textual state/freshness/reasons, immutable history, labels, keyboard controls and responsive layout expose persisted evidence; failed reads show Unknown, action errors survive polling, busy reconciliation excludes controls and editing rejects assertion overrides. See [Contact, interface and delivery](#contact-interface-and-delivery). **Acceptance evidence:** Use the real owner console at 1440 and 390 with healthy, stale, failing and unavailable monitors; edit assertions, force read/action errors and polling, navigate by keyboard and inspect focus, text state, overflow, accessibility tree and axe results.

- **NOTIFY-001 — Authorized installation-wide destinations.** An owner may configure email, signed JSON webhook, or Slack incoming webhook destinations for all monitors in one installation; only scheduled incident transitions generate automatic notifications, and a test send cannot change monitor evidence or incident state. The hosted Worker submits email through the configured Base mail adapter and sends webhooks and Slack without a private runner; a private runner can also claim notification work. Webhook destinations also take WEBHOOK-001's configurable method, content type, custom headers and payload template. Destination credentials are installation-local and are not restored from archives. See [Contact, interface and delivery](#contact-interface-and-delivery) and [Network and credentials](#network-and-credentials). **Acceptance evidence:** Create/rotate/disable email, Slack and signed webhook destinations, cause scheduled failure/recovery and a test send through actual Worker/runner delivery doors; inspect one job per enabled destination, credentials, exact signing, escaping and unchanged health for tests.

- **DELIVERY-001 — Bounded notification acknowledgement.** Each event and destination without receiver-side deduplication (email, Slack incoming webhooks and the other channels DELIVERY-002 names), every test send and every drill gets one automatic send attempt. A RER relay 429 with Retry-After confirms non-acceptance and queues another submission after that delay. For those sends HTTP 500, connection loss, timeout, and unknown outcomes remain uncertain and are not automatically sent again; deduplicating destinations retry and every route can fall back to another contact under DELIVERY-002. Accepted means the configured provider or endpoint acknowledged the request; it does not mean a person received, read, or acted on it. See [Contact, interface and delivery](#contact-interface-and-delivery). **Acceptance evidence:** At authorized provider edges return accepted, proven-unaccepted relay 429, HTTP 500, timeout and connection loss; restart during claims. Only the proven-unaccepted case requeues automatically; uncertain started sends never replay and acceptance remains distinct from receipt.

- **PUBLIC-001 — Public landing and documentation.** At the app origin, `/` is the unauthenticated Lobby, `/docs` is public documentation, `/setup` is first-run setup, and the private operator console and editor are `/app` and `/app/monitor`; private documents require an owner session. The separate marketing site serves `https://run-uptime.com/` and `https://run-uptime.com/run`. The app Lobby at `/` and the marketing landing at the run-site `/` are distinct surfaces. **Acceptance evidence:** Open app-origin Lobby, docs and setup signed out, then sign in for /app and /app/monitor; separately read the marketing site and /run. Their routes remain distinct and private documents reject unsigned access.

- **BOUNDARY-001 — Monitoring ownership and honest delivery claims.** Uptime owns checks, evidence, routing, repeat reminders and monitoring reports. It feeds full paging and responder coordination to [Oncall](https://github.com/just-every/run-oncall), PagerDuty or Splunk On-Call, and public incidents, branded pages and subscriber updates to [Lamp](https://github.com/just-every/run-lamp) or Atlassian Statuspage. Teams, Discord, Telegram, SMS, voice and push are supported target channels under the clauses below. One network label is one vantage; provider acknowledgement never proves human receipt or response. Geographic placement, browser execution and accessibility require their own measured evidence. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure an alert and a sibling handoff, inspect acknowledged/received/responded as separate facts and simulate lost probes/providers; no duplicate network labels, acknowledgement or missing evidence becomes a delivery or availability guarantee.

- **DRILL-001 — Recipient-reported alert drills.** An owner can run an alert drill on one destination: the existing test event with drill wording, sent once through the same path with no automatic resend, plus a one-time confirmation link (random capability, stored only as a bound hash, single use, 24-hour expiry, one drill and one destination). Opening the link shows a page; only a same-origin POST confirms, and unknown, used, expired and foreign links answer 404 alike. The console keeps Submitted, Acknowledged (or Uncertain/Failed with its code) and Recipient confirmed as separate lines, and does not measure whether anyone acted. A confirmed drill is a report by the recipient that one message reached a person at one time. It is never evidence of monitoring, a delivery guarantee or a human response, and it changes no monitor evidence, state or incident. Proof belongs to the destination's current credential: a new address or URL or a rotated secret makes it Never proven, and drill history stays with the installation, so a restored destination is Never proven. See [Alert drills](#alert-drills). **Acceptance evidence:** Drill email, Slack and signed webhook through the actual delivery runtime: GET/HEAD never confirm, same-origin POST confirms once, expired/replayed/foreign links refuse, 500 stays uncertain, rotation resets proof and archives/logs contain no capability or endpoint secret.

- **STARTUP-001 — Required configuration diagnostics.** A shared configuration table validates required and conditional bindings for the Worker, scheduled entry point, local app, preview, setup, deployment and private runner before each begins work. Missing or malformed required values stop the entry point and name the variable without printing its value; a Worker configuration failure returns 503, including at `/health`. Hosted mode requires the selected Base mail provider and its sender configuration. See [Configuration diagnostics](#configuration-diagnostics). **Acceptance evidence:** Start each runtime entry point with required and conditional bindings missing/malformed; diagnostics name bindings without values and health returns 503. Restore settings and recover; exercise D1/ASSETS faults inside the owner-approved three-second readiness response budget.

- **PUBLIC-002 — Declared sessionless paths and rate limits.** Every sessionless route and emitted static path is declared in `seed.json` with its authentication rule and rate-limit class. D1 applies owner-tunable per-client limits in fixed 60-second windows and returns 429 with `Retry-After` after the class limit. A declaration does not bypass route-specific session, runner, heartbeat or development-key checks. Limit values are marked estimated and not load-tested. See [Public paths and rate limits](#public-paths-and-rate-limits) and [Integrity and authority](#integrity-and-authority). **Acceptance evidence:** Enumerate declared sessionless paths against emitted routes, exceed configured public limits, inspect Retry-After, and remove counter storage; each route retains its own authentication checks and admission fails closed.

- **FLOW-001 — Supported isolated browser transactions.** Login, signup and checkout are supported monitored customer paths with explicit intended outcomes, immutable revisions, safe synthetic accounts and scheduled consensus. A constrained editor and isolated Playwright execution share the same evidence and authority rules. Every required outcome must pass; missing browser/credentials is Unknown. Per-run process/profile isolation, sanitized environment, network filtering, hard time/byte limits and credential-origin binding apply to all scripts. Human approval is required for effects beyond an explicitly authorized synthetic transaction. A changed-definition recovery is labelled and never presented as proof of the original outcome. The current constrained-flow implementation is partial; support qualification and the additional FLOW clauses remain required. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure supported login, signup and sandbox checkout journeys, run independent real-browser vantages, break required outcomes and recover through signed results. Wrong credentials, missing elements, server errors, slow pages, private redirects/subresources, extra origins and absent browsers have distinct retained outcomes; weakened definitions cannot silently clear the original incident.

## Target clauses

### Target extensions to existing journeys

- **NETWORK-002 — Explicit TLS trust exceptions for legacy targets.** For self-signed, private-CA or legacy targets, a monitor can pin a buyer-supplied CA certificate or a leaf-certificate SHA-256 fingerprint (the preferred option). It can instead set an explicit per-monitor "ignore certificate errors" option, which an owner enables after a warning. Either exception is labelled on the monitor and in its evidence, and each slot records the TLS error it bypassed. An exception never applies after a redirect to another origin, and request credentials (API-002) are never sent to an unverified certificate unless that monitor's owner has granted it separately. TLS-001 still reports the certificate truthfully, and NETWORK-001's full verification remains the default. UptimeRobot's "ignore SSL errors" setting and its Pingdom equivalent import onto this option and are named in the preview. **Acceptance evidence:** Against a controlled self-signed target, see the default check fail with a TLS error, then pass with a pinned fingerprint and with the ignore option. Rotate the certificate and see the pinned monitor fail. Follow a cross-origin redirect and see verification enforced. Inspect the labels and the bypassed-error evidence, and import an UptimeRobot monitor with "ignore SSL errors" set and see the option in the preview.

  *Status:* done.

- **ARCHIVE-002 — Whole-workspace archives at monitoring scale.** Whole-workspace export, OPS-001 backup and restore handle SCHEDULE-002's scale: 500 monitors on 30-second checks from 3 locations (about 4.3 million attempts a day) with at least 90 days of retained history. The archive streams in bounded chunks under a manifest with per-chunk digests rather than as one 2 MiB document. Restore keeps ARCHIVE-001's validation, inactive-restore and erasure-fencing semantics and resumes after interruption. Exports for a time range or a subset of monitors are available. Any remaining bound is shown in the console before export, and an export over that bound refuses with its counts; it never truncates. **Acceptance evidence:** Populate 500 monitors with 90 days of 30-second, three-location history, export it, interrupt and resume the export, and verify the manifest digests. Restore into an empty installation and compare every table's counts and ARCHIVE-001 transformations. A 120-monitor account exports in full, and a range or subset export matches its selection.

### Monitoring and operations

- **MONITOR-001 — Owned monitor catalog and lifecycle.** An operator organizes monitors by project, environment, service, tags, owner and dependencies; bulk create/edit/pause/resume/clone/search/filter preserve revision and incident history. Every monitor names its intended customer outcome, schedule, locations, assertions, maintenance and alert policy. Pausing never erases downtime. Bulk add takes a pasted list of URLs (one per line) or an uploaded CSV of up to 500 rows. The CSV has a `url` column and optional `name`, `type`, `keyword`, `interval`, `tags` and `group` columns. Settings shared across the batch (type, interval, locations, alert route, tags) fill whatever the rows leave out. A preview without writes marks each row as create, duplicate of an existing monitor, or refused with its reason (an invalid URL, a forbidden destination under NETWORK-001 or a quota). It also shows COST-001's projected traffic and cost. Apply creates the accepted rows in one transaction, as one IMPORT-001-style undoable batch. The monitors start paused unless the owner chooses "enable after create" in the reviewed preview. **Acceptance evidence:** Create production/staging monitors in the console/API, bulk change a selected tag, clone one, pause/resume another and compare revision, ownership and incident history before/after. Paste 50 URLs, including a duplicate, an invalid URL and a private address, with a shared 60-second interval, two locations and a route. The preview marks 47 creates, 1 duplicate and 2 refusals with reasons. Apply with "enable after create" and see 47 monitors checking on their first slot. Undo the batch, and a CSV upload with per-row names and tags produces the same plan.

  *Status:* partial.

- **API-001 — Complete HTTP and API assertions.** HTTP(S) methods, safe redirects, custom headers/body, expected status sets/ranges, keyword presence and absence, response headers, latency, nested JSONPath and regular-expression assertions evaluate the exact saved bundle with bounded redacted diagnostics. A transport 200 cannot pass a failed business assertion. **Acceptance evidence:** Save checks through the editor/API against a controlled endpoint; independently break absent/present keywords, status range, response headers, JSON arrays/nested paths, regex and latency, then inspect the distinct failures and unchanged saved criteria.

- **SLOW-001 — Slow-response alerts distinct from outages.** A monitor can set a slow threshold, separate from any API-001 latency assertion: a response time over N consecutive slots, or a percentile over a window, per location or across locations. Crossing it opens a Slow degradation event with its own route, contacts and notices under ROUTING-001/ROUTING-002, plus a slow-recovered notice. A Slow event is not an incident: it does not count as downtime in availability or SLA figures, and REPORT-001 reports it as slow time. A latency assertion stays a failure for buyers who want slowness to count as down. Response-time thresholds from Pingdom, Better Stack and Site24x7 map onto it during migration. **Acceptance evidence:** Set a 2,000 ms slow threshold over 3 slots on a controlled endpoint and slow it to 3,000 ms. A Slow notice reaches its route, no incident opens, uptime is unchanged and the report shows slow time. Recover and see the slow-recovered notice. A latency assertion on another monitor still fails that monitor as down.

- **API-002 — Authenticated monitoring and secret lifecycle.** Basic, HTTP Digest and bearer, OAuth 2.0 acquisition/refresh, JWT signing and mTLS client certificates use owner-authorized secret references, least scope, expiry/rotation and exact origins. Test accounts and credentials remain under installation/runner custody; auth errors remain distinguishable from application failures and secrets never enter evidence or exports. **Acceptance evidence:** Use each authentication mode against an authorized endpoint, expire/rotate/revoke it and redirect cross-origin; show expected failure/recovery, refusal of foreign references and no planted secret in storage, logs, browser artifacts or archives.

  *Status:* done.

- **API-003 — Multi-step API transactions.** A scheduled backend transaction shares scoped authentication, variables and extracted response values across ordered requests, asserts each business outcome and records per-step timing. Setup, failure and cleanup remain visible; a later success cannot erase an earlier failed requirement. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a login/create/read/delete API journey through the supported configuration door, fail a middle assertion and expire shared auth, then inspect step history and cleanup without leaking extracted secrets.

- **API-004 — Safe setup and teardown.** Browser and API journeys run bounded setup/teardown fixtures using synthetic accounts, Stripe test mode and buyer sandbox resources; cleanup runs after failure or cancellation and leaves durable actionable cleanup debt when it cannot complete. Live purchases, destructive writes or refunds require an exact time-limited grant and spend limit. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a sandbox checkout with setup/teardown, cancel mid-flow and deny cleanup at the network edge; inspect bounded attempts, retained cleanup failure and explicit operator resolution without a live charge.

- **TCP-001 — TCP service checks.** TCP monitors test a chosen host/port, handshake latency and optional bounded banner from selected locations with the same confirmation, evidence and alert policy as HTTP. Refused connections, timeout and probe failure remain distinct. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a TCP monitor through the console/API; open, close and blackhole an authorized port from two locations, then read evidence and trigger/recovery events.

- **PING-001 — ICMP reachability and packet quality.** ICMP ping monitors retain bounded packet loss, round-trip latency and configured thresholds by location for IPv4/IPv6. ICMP blocked by policy is shown explicitly rather than inferred from HTTP success. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure a ping target, vary loss and latency, disable ICMP and stop a probe; inspect protocol-specific results and Unknown probe coverage.

- **DNS-001 — DNS records and change detection.** Checks query selected recursive and authoritative resolvers for A, AAAA, CNAME, MX, TXT, NS, SOA, SRV and CAA records, expected values, absence/change and response time. NXDOMAIN, SERVFAIL, propagation differences and monitor infrastructure faults are distinguishable; owner-approved expected changes suppress only their own condition. **Acceptance evidence:** Save DNS monitors via the owner door, change/remove an expected record and induce resolver disagreement/failure; inspect raw bounded record evidence, confirmed alerts, maintenance and recovery without treating a missing answer as success.

- **TLS-001 — SSL certificate and chain monitoring.** TLS checks verify hostname/SNI, certificate chain/trust, validity, expiry and renewal on HTTPS or configured TLS ports from each location. Staged configurable expiry warnings name the certificate and accountable owner, track renewal and resolve only after a newly observed valid chain. **Acceptance evidence:** Monitor a controlled TLS service; present an expiring certificate, broken chain, wrong name and then renewed certificate, advance the clock and inspect deduplicated warnings, location evidence and renewal resolution.

- **DOMAIN-001 — Domain registration expiry.** Domain monitors use RDAP and authorized registrar/WHOIS data, retain source/freshness, warn the owner before registration expiry and follow through until renewal is independently observed. Redacted, unavailable or conflicting registry data is Unknown; certificate renewal is not domain renewal. **Acceptance evidence:** Add a domain in the console, drive authorized registry-edge responses through approaching expiry, unavailable data, conflicting dates and renewal; inspect distinct warnings, source times and resolved follow-up.

- **UDP-001 — UDP request and response checks.** Bounded UDP checks validate configured payload/response and timing on an authorized host/port with anti-abuse traffic limits and location consensus. No response is not treated as proof of an open port. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a UDP monitor, return matching/mismatching payloads, drop packets and remove probe authority; inspect timeout versus assertion versus runner failures and bounded outbound traffic.

- **MAIL-001 — SMTP and mailbox protocol checks.** SMTP/STARTTLS, POP3/POP3S and IMAP/IMAPS checks verify connection, TLS, banner, authentication and optional synthetic send/receive transaction with a dedicated test mailbox. Message IDs, timing and cleanup are retained without mailbox content or passwords; a successful SMTP accept is distinct from mailbox receipt. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure each protocol via the owner door against authorized mail edges, break authentication/TLS and delay receipt; inspect per-step alerts, receiver confirmation and removal of synthetic messages.

- **HEARTBEAT-002 — Cron and job heartbeat monitoring.** A buyer monitors a scheduled job by choosing either an expected period (30 seconds to 31 days) or a five-field cron expression with an IANA time zone (US zones offered first, daylight-saving gaps and repeats handled explicitly), plus a grace period (0 seconds to 24 hours). A run is missed when no success ping arrives by its expected time plus grace: the scheduled evaluation due at that instant confirms Failing, never earlier, and a later success recovers it labelled as a late run. The job can report its own failure without waiting for the window: a `/fail` or `/fail/<exit code>` ping schedules an immediate evaluation, so the incident opens within SCHEDULE-002's alert bound with the exit code and a bounded redacted output excerpt (request body up to 10 KiB, owner masks and EVIDENCE-002 secret patterns applied); a later success recovers it. An optional `/start` ping measures run duration: a run exceeding the owner's maximum duration alerts as Running too long, a start with no finish is a missed run, and every completed run's duration appears in history and REPORT-001. Receipts accept GET, HEAD and POST so curl, wget, PowerShell and HTTP-only schedulers work, either through a URL-capability form (rotatable, hashed at rest, rate-limited and labelled replay-unprotected, matching incumbent heartbeat URLs) or the sequence-protected header form; both keep HEARTBEAT-001's revision, ingress and import rules, and no ping by itself establishes Healthy. The console and API generate copy-paste snippets for crontab, curl/wget, systemd timers, Kubernetes CronJob, GitHub Actions and Windows Task Scheduler that wrap a command so start, success and failure with exit code are all reported. UptimeRobot, Pingdom and Better Stack heartbeat periods and grace map through the MIGRATE clauses with newly issued URLs. **Acceptance evidence:** Through the console and API configure `0 2 * * *` in America/New_York with 15-minute grace; skip one run and see the alert open at 02:15 local time and not before, including across a DST change. Send `/fail/3` with output containing a planted token and see an immediate incident naming exit code 3 with the token masked, then recover with a success. Send `/start`, then success after 40 minutes against a 30-minute maximum, and see Running too long plus the recorded duration. Receive GET, HEAD and POST pings; run each generated snippet unmodified against a real shell or scheduler edge; rotate the URL and prove the old URL, a replayed header sequence and an imported receipt cannot establish Healthy.

  *Status:* partial.

- **SCHEDULE-001 — Explicit cadence, retries and confirmation.** Each revision records interval, timeout, retry budget, freshness, failure/recovery slot thresholds, fixed selected probes and assignments. Scheduled work cannot count retries as independent slots, invent missed checks or let manual runs alter cadence. - **SCHEDULE-002 — Interval floor, on-time execution and alert latency.** Every protocol monitor (HTTP/API, keyword, TCP, ping, DNS, UDP, mail and heartbeat) can run every 30 seconds from each selected managed location, with 60 seconds as the default; TLS and domain expiry checks run at least hourly and browser transactions as often as every minute. ## Declared external data flows and baseline replacements

`seed.json → externals` declares the source-level outbound data categories. Public HTTP(S) monitors and status-only canaries resolve each public target, validate every redirect hop, and send the configured method, path/query, allowed headers and request body to the target. A request credential is sent only to its exact owner-authorized monitor origin; cross-origin redirects strip it, custom headers and body. Ordinary checks read bounded response status, headers and body for assertions without retaining raw bodies in observations; the EVIDENCE-002 target adds a separately stored redacted failure excerpt, declared here before it ships. A canary is a status-only `GET` with no credential, custom headers or body and reads zero response-body bytes. DNS queries reveal only the hostnames being resolved plus source network address and lookup timing to the resolver configured for the runner.

TCP and TLS monitors connect to the owner's public host and port at its validated address: TCP sends nothing and may read up to 512 bytes of banner (256 printable characters kept); TLS sends a ClientHello naming the host and keeps certificate subject, issuer, names, validity and fingerprint. DNS monitors send the monitored name and type to the selected public resolvers (Cloudflare, Google) over HTTPS and, when chosen, one non-recursive query over TCP to the zone's authoritative server; bounded normalised records are kept. Domain monitors send the domain name to IANA's RDAP bootstrap, its registry and the registrar record the registry links, over HTTPS; the expiry date, source and update time, status values and redaction or conflict are kept. These flows are declared in `seed.json` as `public-protocol-targets`, `dns-over-https-resolvers` and `rdap-registries`.

Discovery is read-only and runs on the owner's runner; a hosted demo, which has no runner, runs the same crawl and limits in the Worker over public GET/HEAD requests without credentials. It may follow any validated public redirect from the initial URL; subsequent crawls are bounded to the final/input registrable domains and linked status hosts, subject to robots and request/time/body limits. It may request robots files, permitted pages, JSON/spec documents, conventional API hosts and status hosts. Bounded response content is parsed in runner memory. Only validated definitions, hostnames, URLs, short notes and request/timing summaries return to the Worker; raw HTML, response headers and page bodies do not. The Worker stores only the bounded derived result. No supplied HTML is rendered as console HTML.

Browser flows send ordinary browser requests to owner-declared top-level origins and any public subresource or WebSocket host reached by a page; redirects and subresources pass the same destination-filtering proxy, which blocks private, loopback, link-local and metadata addresses. The destinations receive request paths, browser headers, source address and timing, and cookies that a destination itself sets. The isolated runner browser processes page content locally; only the sanitized step trace returns through the control plane, not screenshots or page bodies.

The private runner sends signed enrolment, capabilities, claims, contact timing, signed attempt evidence and derived discovery results to the owner-configured control-plane origin. A notification claim can return the selected endpoint URL, signing credential and event content to the runner, which sends the one authorized webhook or Slack request and returns its bounded delivery outcome. The hosted Worker submits queued email through the selected Base mail adapter and sends webhooks and Slack itself. ## Stored evidence and independent failure agreement (C3)

A slot outcome and scheduled confirmation/recovery streaks are pure projections of stored attempts, recalculated on each arrival in chronological slot order. A derived checkpoint folds only expired acceptance windows and keeps exact, unclamped counters; complete slots still accepting late attempts remain mutable. Reads re-evaluate current freshness without advancing the checkpoint counters. Failing requires terminal target failures from two distinct runner network labels, or one failing vantage with its passing canary in the same slot. A failed canary counts its network absent; its failure remains Degraded. Missing/expired evidence is Unknown; recovery requires complete fresh passing coverage. Same-label runners count once. New enabled production monitor settings refuse configurations incapable of reaching Failing: a public monitor on one network label with no external check URL of its own takes the installation's External check URL (an owner-only setting on Runner / archive, seeded with `https://example.org/`) into its saved revision, so it is refused only when the setting is absent. An external check on the monitored site itself (the same hostname, ignoring case and a leading `www.`) is refused whether it is the default or the owner's own, because it would fail whenever the monitor does and a failure could never be confirmed. Revisions are immutable, so changing the setting applies to monitors saved afterwards and never rewrites a saved one. The setting is installation-local and does not travel in archives; the URL a revision carries does. Assignment-free historical revisions keep their saved terminal-probe failure quorum until the owner saves a new revision; the overview and count-only owner notices flag the need for a second independent network or an external check URL. `tests/resilience/attempts.mjs` proves these flows through signed Worker routes and real probes at local network edges.

## Per-runner assigned attempts (C4)

Each runner has an independently revocable key and a declared network label. A revision assigns each probe to a runner. The immutable attempt identity is slot/probe/attempt number; only that assigned, unrevoked runner can submit it inside the acceptance window. The first accepted copy wins. Results have no slot-wide lease owner and may contain any assigned subset. Stored evidence, recalculated state and incident events commit atomically under a generation guard. Migration preserves the existing runner as the first row. C5's pinned resolution remains unchanged. `tests/resilience/attempts.mjs` covers migration, signatures, assignments, duplicates, expiry, order and revocation through public routes.


## Evidence and state

Every scheduled slot and attempt retains its monitor revision, policy, selected probes, runner version, start/completion, status, assertion results and error category. Retries are attempts inside a slot and never erase an earlier failure or increment scheduled confirmation/recovery streaks. Raw target response bodies and probe-request secrets are not retained in observations (EVIDENCE-002's redacted failure snapshot is stored apart from them); notification destination credentials remain installation-local and are omitted from portable archives.

The 24-hour and 7-day history coverage includes complete scheduled results across revisions, evaluated with each immutable revision’s settings and bounded by its next revision. Maintenance and paused periods are shown separately from observed and Unknown time; historical results cannot establish current-revision health. Last check retains the latest completed scheduled observation across revisions.

Target evidence, coverage, and notification delivery are independent. Healthy requires fresh, complete selected-probe coverage and the configured recovery threshold. Incomplete passing coverage is Unknown. A fresh failure without independent-vantage agreement is Degraded; meeting the C3 agreement and confirmation threshold is Failing even when coverage is incomplete. Expired evidence becomes Unknown without silently resolving an open incident. Recovery requires complete fresh passing scheduled slots. Runner errors and absent observations remain distinct from target failure. Scheduled incident transitions and their destination jobs are committed with the corresponding observation and state change; a test send is separate from that history.

One-time and weekly maintenance windows use IANA time zones, explicit DST gap/first-repeat handling, previewed monitor selections and revision-guarded group apply/cancel with audit. Suppression preserves execution, observations and target state; an execution-pause window stops new checks while retaining in-flight evidence. Timeline and reports show maintenance separately with revision-bounded denominators. After a window expires or is cancelled, actual completed evidence determines state and a real completed slot may open a still-confirmed failure. Recovery requires a previously open trigger, preventing orphan recoveries. Pending means stored locally. Manual checks do not change scheduled cadence, confirmation or recovery.

## Integrity and authority

Each Node runner signs purpose-separated enrolment and results with its own scoped key. Identity, declared network label, revocation and credentials stay installation-local. Legacy key-only clients resolve to the migrated first runner; they have no authority over other runners' probes. See C4 above.

Slot identity is monitor/revision/due time; attempt identity includes slot/probe/attempt. Manual requests have distinct identities. The acceptance window is bounded; expired, foreign or contradictory attempts are rejected and the first accepted copy wins. Edited or paused inflight work remains historical evidence and cannot change current state or events. Missed cadence is counted without fabricated catchup results. A scheduled check is due no sooner than one interval after the monitor's previous scheduled check was claimed and after its first attempt reported starting; after an edit, the shorter of the old and new intervals applies, so a late check moves later due times back rather than running checks closer together; equal runner and Worker clocks make the reported-start floor exact. Backward clock movement waits for due time; future evidence cannot establish health.

Observation, state and outbox writes share one durable transaction. Migration history is permanent. Completion verifies the immutable definition, fixed probe denominator, exact assertion bundle, outcome, clock bounds and transport limits. Live latency validation uses the larger retained elapsed measure. Historical archive compatibility does not relax live validation.

Every private document, mutation, observation read and export requires server-side operator or scoped runner authority. Missing configuration fails closed. Browser mutations bind the configured origin and reject cross-site requests. Loopback is not authentication. Shared Base operator-auth owns operator credentials and sessions; product policy must not silently grant additional roles. Signed reporting identifies the holder of the runner key; it does not prove an uncompromised runner.

## Network and credentials

Private-runner public HTTP(S) checks validate every A/AAAA answer and redirect hop, reject unsafe or mixed address sets, pin numeric sockets and retain original Host and SNI. Certificate verification is the default; NETWORK-002’s owner-saved fingerprint, private CA or acknowledged ignore exception applies only to the initial HTTPS origin, records bounded certificate/error facts and never authorizes credentials. Cross-origin redirects restore default verification. These exceptions require a private runner; the hosted Worker refuses them. Proxy environments are bypassed. HTTPS downgrade is rejected; an origin change strips credentials, custom headers, request body and mutating method. DNS, redirects, body and retries have bounded execution. Responses are limited to 64 KiB; compressed responses are rejected. An external check asserts status 200 only, so it stops at the response headers and neither reads nor keeps the body, however large the page; every other guarantee here applies to it unchanged. Independent network isolation remains an operating requirement. Hosted HTTP(S) and keyword monitors without runner-local credentials default to the Cloudflare Worker. Owner-console requests prompt due work; its one-minute Cron schedule continues background work, but initial Cloudflare activation can take up to about two hours. The Worker validates all public DNS answers and every redirect and uses Cloudflare’s public Internet fetch transport with certificate verification, no private-network binding, no cached probe response and the same time, body and assertion bounds. Numeric socket pinning is the private runner’s transport guarantee. The Worker is one network vantage; it uses the same configured canary and scheduled evidence rules and cannot supply independent regional agreement. TCP, DNS, TLS and domain checks also run in the Worker or an assigned private runner. Worker TCP cannot reach Cloudflare addresses, Worker TLS cannot inspect a TLS 1.3-only service, and a registry without RDAP has no WHOIS fallback; these cases remain Unknown as the protocol Status rows describe. Browser flows and checks using runner-local credentials require an assigned private runner.

Synthetic checks retain a separate live process/custody grant, exact path/port identity, expiry, GET/HEAD-only and no-redirect boundary. Grant authority is rechecked on every attempt.

Request headers/body, Basic, Digest and bearer authentication, OAuth 2.0 client-credentials or refresh-token acquisition, JWT signing and mTLS client certificates/keys use protected runner-local regular files with exact monitor-ID and origin binding, expiry and rotation checks. OAuth token requests use their configured HTTPS token endpoint; refresh state stays local. Credential failures remain distinct runner errors, and neither token material nor client private keys enter evidence or exports. Credential authority comes from the authenticated task envelope while its assigned probe and acceptance window are valid; another monitor on the same runner cannot use the same reference to obtain the credential. Setup runs numbered local-file migration 0001: valid unbound files used by exactly one existing monitor bind automatically, counting paused monitors; shared and unused files remain unbound with explicit owner instructions. Scheduled checks record bounded non-secret binding diagnostics and never bind files. Missing or invalid references yield runner errors without falling back to unauthenticated requests. Raw secrets and raw response bodies never enter observations, alerts or archives; only EVIDENCE-002's redacted excerpt may be stored, separately, and travel in archives. Portable historical references are not receiving-installation execution authority.

Heartbeat tokens are owner-issued, hashed at rest, scoped to an immutable revision and rotated on issuance. Server receipt time and strictly increasing sequences prevent replay in the current header form; HEARTBEAT-002's URL-capability form is labelled replay-unprotected. Ingress alone cannot change state. A scheduled slot freezes an installation-local receipt snapshot; signed completion must match it. Missing, stale, future, paused, revision-mismatched or imported receipts cannot establish Healthy.

## Flows

The current constrained browser-flow code is not yet qualified as supported. FLOW-001 through FLOW-004 define the supported target; the following describes the existing constrained implementation and its retained safety obligations.

A flow is validated by the same function in the Worker, the definitions engine and the runner. Steps are `goto`, `click`, `fill` (a `credentialRef`, never a value), `waitFor` and `expect`; a target is exactly one of a plain CSS selector, text, label, or a role with its name. Required outcomes have an id and an expectation, and every one must be observed after the last step. Unknown step kinds and fields, scripts, other selector engines, secrets, intervals under 5 minutes, more than 20 steps or 8 outcomes, and more than 5 flows in an installation are refused with the entry named. Bounds per run: 60 seconds of wall time, 8 top-level pages, 5 MiB of response bytes, an 8 KiB trace; exceeding one fails the run and names it.

The runner serves a flow only through `runners/src/flow/`: its own process, a fresh 0700 profile deleted afterwards, an environment holding no inherited credential, proxy setting or Node hook, and the named credentials passed over a pipe. Chromium's only route to the network is a filtering proxy inside the runner; it resolves each destination itself, connects to the address it checked, refuses private, loopback, link-local and metadata addresses for pages, redirects, subresources and WebSockets, and reaches the local fixture only through the synthetic grant. A page may load top-level pages only on the origins the flow names and may not open a second window.

A flow runs a probe like any monitor, so incidents need the same vantage agreement. Failures keep distinct reasons (expected text or element missing, no element for a click, an error status, the wall limit, a forbidden address, a second origin, a missing outcome); a runner-side problem (no browser, a missing credential) is a runner error and the flow is Unknown. An observation's `flow.digest` must equal the digest of the flow in its slot's revision. When the revision now running requires less than the revision that was current when the open incident began, a pass recovers that incident only with the changed-definition label in the state, the recovery event and the console, and the revision history shows when the required outcomes shrank. Credentials are runner-local files bound to an exact origin and appear in no table, archive, export, log, trace or audit record. The flow tables are the existing monitor, revision, slot and observation tables; no table was added.

## Alert drills

A drill exists to check, one destination at a time, that an alert reaches somebody who can act. **Run alert drill** queues the test event with drill wording (`eventType` `uptime.notification.drill`, `kind` `test`, `incidentKey` null) for that destination's current credential. Disabled destinations can be drilled, as they can be tested. The one automatic attempt and the uncertain-never-resent rule are DELIVERY-001's. The message names who started the drill and when, and carries a link to `/alert-drill` holding a random 256-bit capability. Email has it as a text line and an HTML link, Slack as a message line, and the signed JSON webhook as an extra top-level `drill` object (`startedBy`, `startedAt`, `expiresAt`, `confirmUrl`) on drill events only. Incident events retain their immutable context; configurable webhook requests and payloads use WEBHOOK-001’s exact-byte signing. The default JSON envelope and Lamp-compatible signing remain available.

D1 stores the capability's SHA-256, bound to the drill, the destination and its credential. A copy sealed under the installation key lives only until the single send is over, and the runner reads it only while it holds that delivery's claim. The stored event, the stored webhook/Slack envelope, the archive and the console reads never contain the link. Opening the link (GET or HEAD) shows a page naming only the destination kind and the drill time. Only a same-origin form POST confirms, compared in constant time and accepted once within 24 hours. Unknown, used, expired and foreign links answer the ordinary 404 page, and the path is declared in `seed.json` under its own rate-limit class. The recipient needs no sign-in.

The console shows, per destination, "Last proven" (the latest confirmed drill for the current credential), "Not proven in 30 days" or "Never proven" in words, beside the latest drill's three lines:

- **Submitted** is when Uptime started the one send. Before then it says the drill is queued, or why nothing was sent.
- **Acknowledged** is when the provider or endpoint answered and with what code, or Uncertain or Failed with the code. Acknowledgement is not inbox receipt.
- **Recipient confirmed** shows the time someone used the link, reported by the recipient. Until then it is pending, and after 24 hours it reads "not confirmed within 24 hours" with nothing sent again.

Whether anyone acted is not measured. The first-run page lists any destinations already present with the same proof words. Drill history (`alert_drills`) is installation-local: archives carry the drill's delivery history like any test send, but never the drill rows, so restored destinations start Never proven. Erasing a destination erases its drills; erasing an operator removes the operator reference from the drills they started. Unmeasured: no hosted drill to a real inbox or channel has been run (see ACCEPTANCE.md).

## Portability and retention

Versioned archives include definitions, immutable observations, state, product outbox events, notification history for source records that remain, erasure receipts, the permanent anonymised action audit, source identity and erasure journal, and an integrity digest. Legacy development sign-in records are installation-local, not archive data, and remain until their associated operator is erased or local reset; no captured-mail viewer or new sign-in capture is exposed. Restore validates bounded UTF8 bytes, schema, version, semantics and hash before transaction and requires empty product data. Existing receiving-owner, runner-registration and idle-contact records do not make product data nonempty and remain unchanged by restore. Imported monitors gain new paused current revisions; leases are interrupted and alerts quarantined. Derived attempt checkpoints, request credential values, heartbeat authority, sessions, keys, grants, contact, webhook and Slack URLs, and signing keys are installation-local and are not imported; destination definitions and retained notification histories are portable. A local CLI receiving checkout must first run `rer setup --owner-email EMAIL --non-interactive` to establish its owner and settings; setup leaves product data empty for import. No imported event or receipt automatically replays. A digest checks changed bytes, not trustworthy provenance. Observation metadata, monitor definitions, destination and delivery history, audit history, and legacy development sign-in records have no automatic age/count deletion. Short-lived authorization rows can also expire logically without being deleted; expired discovery jobs are marked expired and retained. Drill expiry/confirmation clears only the one-time capability ciphertext/hash while retaining the drill row. Owner rotation/deletion retires endpoint credentials by scrubbing their secret material after active sends finish while keeping the credential/history row. The notification bridge removes an `integration_attempts` row only when it proves that the exact attempt never reached the send boundary; its delivery reservation and job remain. Only aged `oa_sessions`, `oa_limits`, `login_attempts` and `public_rate_limits` rows decreased, and all other preexisting row identities survived the measured cron/lazy-expiry paths and Worker restart. This scan covers its listed execution classes, not every active automatic cleanup transition or optional provider integration. View limits (100 history slots and 200 outbox entries), monitor limits and archive limits are not deletion rules or measured storage capacity. Storage growth remains an operator responsibility. Deletion must never silently improve the configured denominator or historical health.

### Erasure and audit

Monitor erasure removes its revisions, slots, observations, derived attempt checkpoints, heartbeat claims and authority, incident events, related notification jobs and deliveries, and archive-history copies. Destination erasure removes the destination, all credential versions, dependent notification jobs, delivery attempts and outcomes, replay entries and matching archive-history copies. Operator erasure removes that operator's sessions, tokens, invitations, legacy development sign-in credentials and messages. For the original owner, the bootstrap claim keeps its singleton and claim time but replaces the operator reference with an unrelated anonymous UUID. Its receipt counts that row as anonymised, and the source journal fences its original primary key. The replacement commits in the same guarded batch as account deletion; setup remains claimed and receiving-installation authority remains local. The audit remains separate from the erased entity: its subject reference is opaque, and actor references for an erased operator become anonymised. Erasure writes a durable receipt with per-table counts and completion time in the same D1 batch as its audit record, deletion and Base portability erasure-fence statements.

The audit includes destructive actions and automated notification attempts, including test sends, plus operator authentication-mail provider attempts and legacy development capture history. The mail sender records the feature audit before attempting the provider request. Actor and subject fields are identifiers or feature names, never user-supplied names, emails, URLs or secrets. The audit travels in product archives. A later erasure journal makes restore refuse old audit actor references for an erased operator. Older offline archive bytes and external messages already sent cannot be changed by a later local erasure; a request already in progress may still reach its provider. Uptime does not use R2 or accept uploads, so there are no uploaded attachments to remove.

## Contact, interface and delivery

Authenticated runner contact records control-plane receipt time for accepted scoped claims, including idle claims, and accepted signed results. Owner reads, health checks and rejected requests do not refresh it. Fresh means within 120 seconds; future or unverifiable time is stale. Contact does not prove process liveness or target success. Restart retains it, archives omit it, and failed overview reads label contact unavailable with any prior timestamp clearly historical.

The interface exposes textual state, freshness, reasons, immutable revisions and manual provenance, with labels, keyboard controls and responsive layout. Failed reads show Unknown and last successful refresh. Action errors persist across polling; busy controls stay excluded through reconciliation. Editing must preserve configured checks; duplicated reserved typed assertion keys are rejected rather than silently overriding criteria. No fabricated uptime figure or color-only state is acceptable.

Email, signed webhook and Slack destinations are installation-local definitions. Confirmed incident triggers select recipients from the first non-inherited policy in monitor → service → group → installation order, using the monitor’s current service/group labels in the event transaction. Installation defaults to all enabled destinations or an explicit selection; narrower scopes inherit or select their own list, and an empty selection explicitly means no recipients. Previews show selected/excluded recipients, disabled or missing destinations, unavailable credentials or mail configuration, and affected monitors including narrower overrides. Apply requires the reviewed digest and current revisions, refuses stale inputs and records durable route-change history and audit. Recovery keeps the original trigger’s destination and credential and waits for that trigger to be accepted; rejected or uncertain triggers cannot authorize recovery, and newly selected contacts receive no recovery for an incident they missed. Disabled, erased or rotated contacts do not silently count as covered. Teams, environment/severity/region/origin and business/quiet-hour rules, delays/repeats/retry/fallbacks, portable route settings/history and expiry/renewal routing remain ROUTING-001/ROUTING-002/DELIVERY-002 gaps. Expiry and renewal notices still use installation-wide destinations. Route settings and detailed route history stay local; archives retain opaque audit entries. The hosted Worker submits email through the configured Base mail adapter and sends webhooks and Slack without a private runner; a private runner can also claim notification work. The one-time test action queues a distinct test event and does not change monitor state. Only scheduled incident transitions create trigger and recovery notifications; maintenance suppresses these events, and manual checks do not create them. Portable archives include destination kind, name, enabled setting and available email address, but exclude webhook and Slack URLs, signing keys and other credentials. Restored destinations are disabled until the receiving owner reauthorizes and enables them.
